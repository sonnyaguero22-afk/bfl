# Buckaroo Football League site

A fan site for an 8-team ESPN fantasy league of friends (founded 2023, 6 teams through 2025, 8 from 2026). Sonny, the owner, is the commissioner and the person you work with. The championship is the Buckaroo Bowl (Roman numerals, Bowl I = 2023); the consolation game that decides last place is the Bum Bowl. This repo is public: never commit secrets, the lore, or personal details beyond what the site already shows.

## How it runs

GitHub Actions (`.github/workflows/update.yml`) is scheduled every 15 minutes, but GitHub only fires it every few hours, so on game days each run starts the next itself (`check-games.js next`: about every 5 minutes while NFL games are being played, about every 15 from 4 hours before a kickoff until 5 hours after, hourly when a game is within a day; after Monday night it waits for 9:02am ET Tuesday so the power rankings come out on time; otherwise the chain stops and the schedule takes over). `check-games.js` decides whether a scheduled run updates (around games, or any time between 7am and 11pm ET); manual, push and chained runs always update. Each run checks out the latest `main`. Each update:

1. `fetch.js current` downloads the season from ESPN (needs the `ESPN_S2` and `ESPN_SWID` secrets) into `data/` (raw files, never committed; they contain ESPN account IDs). Past seasons' NFL schedules are saved once to `archive/nfl_<year>.json`.
2. `build.js` builds `public/` (the site) and `site/data.js`: seasons, odds (`odds.js`), this week's player-level simulation (`players.js`), story and post times (`when.js`). Finished seasons are archived to `archive/<year>.json`.
3. `news.js` writes missing stories with Claude Opus (`claude-opus-5-5`), at most 16 per run; stories are saved once in `news/` and never rewritten unless deleted.
4. `live.js` writes live social posts with Claude Sonnet (`claude-sonnet-5-5`) when something happened in games or roster moves; at most 2 calls an hour and 20 a day.
5. A second build, then the workflow commits `archive/`, `history/` and `news/` and deploys to GitHub Pages.

Secrets: `ESPN_S2`, `ESPN_SWID`, `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`, `LEAGUE_LORE` (original nicknames and inside jokes), and `LEAGUE_LORE_II` (later additions, read as LEAGUE_LORE_EXTRA; they override the original). Every run prints a notice with both lore lengths.

## Working from a cloud session

- The session can't reach ESPN or the live site, and can't read Actions logs or secrets. Check runs through annotations instead: `build.js`, `news.js` and `live.js` print `::notice::` and `::warning::` lines (lore lengths, trades found, byes and outs, the playoff picture, each week's win chances with ESPN's, Ones to Watch, same-game correlations). Read them with `gh api repos/sonnyaguero22-afk/bfl/check-runs/<job id>/annotations`.
- Start a run by hand with `gh api -X POST repos/sonnyaguero22-afk/bfl/actions/workflows/update.yml/dispatches -f ref=main`; a manual run may write the week's power rankings early, but never the preview or final word (they wait for lineups).
- Local builds only have the archived seasons (2023 to 2025), not the season in progress. Test site changes by building, copying `public/` to a scratch folder, faking data there (for example marking a 2025 week live), and rendering with Playwright at desktop and 360 to 400px widths; check for page errors and horizontal scroll.
- `sitecode.js` runs the site's own script (`public/index.html`, after a build) in node with a stand-in page, so `news.js` can reuse site calculations (e.g. `run("ltClassFacts('founding')")`) instead of copies that drift.
- Work on `main` and push; every push runs the workflow and deploys. Rewriting a story means deleting its JSON in `news/` and pushing; each one costs API credits, so ask before rewriting many.

## News

Story types in `news.js`, each dated in-world by `when.js`:

| Type | When | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| recap | after Monday night's game | awards, Trade Watch, playoff stakes, team bios, social; `collapses` (`collapses` in `news.js`): a team eliminated that week after peaking at 60%+ playoff odds, with its record and odds at the peak and its record since, told as a storyline (a fitting team-name pun allowed once; Sonny wanted the 2026 fumblers' 3-0, 88% start called out if they miss) |
| rankings | Tuesday 9am ET | NFL.com-style power rankings, own tab (News, Power Rankings); a green ▲ or red ▼ with the spots moved (gray dash if unchanged) under each rank number |
| preview | 2 hours before the week's first NFL kickoff | storylines plus a four-person pundit panel picking every game (graded); win chances assume holes get filled, which are mentioned in passing at most |
| final | Sunday, 75 minutes before the 1pm games | short Sonnet follow-up: lineup moves and win chance changes since the preview, Thursday's points, holes still unfilled; real manager quotes from `QUOTES` (keyed by story id, used word for word; if he benches the player the quote is about, the story says so and `live.js` posts a BENCHED reaction) |
| trade | as soon as a trade is seen | before/after odds from frozen week-end snapshots; each player's whole season including free-agent weeks (`traded_<year>.json` from `fetch.js`), his last three weeks, how the other side got him (`howGot`: pickup, trade or draft round), and his past managers and any reunion (`stints`, `reunion`, `formerManagers`: from every week's rosters since 2023), so pickups flipped into trades and homecomings become storylines; recaps get pickups' reunions and previews the week's best revenge game, if any (`revengeGames`: only fresh ones, left that manager this season, last season after 3+ weeks, or within three seasons after 14+ weeks, so the pool doesn't grow as the league ages; projected 8+, no kickers or defenses; Sonny found a revenge angle in every game too much) |
| veto | within minutes of a vetoed trade (fresh ones only, under two days old) | breaking-news story, Dana Whitfield's scoop leads the social posts; `vetoed` in `build.js` groups ESPN's trade records by `relatedTransactionId` and keeps groups with a VETOED status or TRADE_VETO record and nothing executed (declined or withdrawn proposals were never public and stay out); a notice lists the trade record types and statuses each run; `vetoFacts` reuses `tradeFacts` without odds |
| grades | after week 14's games are final, and again once the season is done (2026 on) | Report Card: a letter grade, headline and blurb for every team (`gradesFacts`: record, points and their rank, luck from all-play expected wins, last five, draft-day vs end odds, trades and pickups; the final version adds each team's playoff or consolation-ladder games and finish and shows the regular-season grade beside the new one; `gradesHtml`) |
| card | the Tuesday a week after each Buckaroo Bowl (every finished season, 2023 on; written only once that date has passed) | postseason Report Card, replacing the old end-of-season grades: a season grade, headline, summary, strengths, weaknesses, a management verdict (well, over-, under- or mismanaged) with sub-grades for draft, waivers, trades and lineups, and 2 to 4 fixes for next season (redraft, so approach not players); `cardFacts` adds position-group ranks, top starters, draft, pickup and trade points, bench points, lineup efficiency against the best possible lineup (`optimal`) and losses the bench would have flipped, plus the season's awards; `cardHtml` shows an At a Glance table and a card per team; linked from the season overview |
| draft | each season | draft-night coverage, no hindsight |
| class | when a Laugh Tale class exists | induction story for each Hall of Fame class (`class-founding`, `class-<year>`), shown on its class page (`#lt-<founding|year>`), not in the feed; facts come from the site's own code via `sitecode.js` |
| bowl | when a season ends | wiki article for each Buckaroo Bowl and Bum Bowl (`bowl-<year>`, `bum-<year>`), shown on its game page, not in the feed |
| review, history, profile | when a season ends | season review, the league history (History tab), manager profiles; the review gets `lineup_twists` (`lineupTwists`: games a lineup call decided, a winner's benched player below zero who'd have tied or lost it with the spot empty or projected at least as high as the starter, or a loser's empty spot the bench would have won; flagged `decided_the_race` when the two teams finished within a game across the playoff or last-place line, like Sonny's empty D/ST spot beating Kai by 1 in 2026 week 3 with the Eagles' -1 on his bench) |
| live | during games, on roster moves and on injury news | `live.js`, shown only in the social feed; reacts to touchdowns, turnovers and big plays, and knows which starters' games are over; INJURY NEWS any day when a rostered player who matters (a regular starter or a top-three scorer on his team) is newly doubtful, out, suspended or on IR, ruled out for the season, has his expected return moved (a setback or ahead of schedule) or comes back from an absence (state keeps status, return date and season flag per player), with his share of the team's points, the record and playoff odds, so a star on IR in a playoff race sets off his fans (melodrama, never self-harm jokes) and a role player barely registers. Injury status comes from `rostered_<year>.json` (`fetch.js`, kona_player_info for ONTEAM players; the box score views leave it out, so in-game injury posts never had it before November 2026), on rosters as `inj`, `injSeason`, `injBack`, `injNote`; the fantasy views carry no return dates, so `injBack` and `injNote` (ESPN's one-line comment) come from ESPN's NFL injury report (`nfl_injuries.json` from `fetch.js`, matched by name; a notice counts its players and return dates), and a notice lists the injured each run; stars a team is playing without right now (`missingStars` in `news.js`: a top-three scorer or regular starter out, suspended or on IR, with when he last played, his return date and ESPN's note) go to every live batch as background, so a fan can miss one now and then (Sonny wanted Habib's fans yearning for Puka), and to the recap as `missing_stars` |

The News tab has filter chips (`NEWS_KINDS`, `newsKind`): All, Recaps, Previews (with final words), Trades (with vetoes), Report Cards (grades and postseason cards) and Season (reviews and drafts); a chip shows only when it has stories. League institutions have One Piece names, used now and then like the Baratie (`SYSTEM`): a vetoed trade is a Buster Call (the veto story's label and headline), the waiver wire is Impel Down (dropped players sent there, pickups break them out), draft night is the Reverie, the league office and commissioner rulings are the World Government, and the Marines enforce the rules (the Bum Bowl fine). Writing rules live in `news.js` (`SYSTEM`): use only the facts given; running jokes and labels are rare; first names and everyday nicknames mixed naturally; Zoe's nickname is always written Z² (also enforced in `build.js`). All coverage comes from GLSN, the Grand Line Sports Network (`STAFF`, `GLSN`, `BYLINE` in `news.js`; `GLSN_STAFF`, `BYLINE`, `letterAva` in `site/index.html`, kept in step): parody minor One Piece characters, never quoting the series, each story under a byline and written lightly in that voice (`by` on the story): Pandaman recaps, Duval power rankings, Haredas previews and final words, Absalom trades and vetoes (he breaks them), Gaimon the draft, Morgans reviews, report cards, history, profiles, Bowls and Laugh Tale classes. Each staffer and the network's own @GLSN (kind "network", ESPN-style alerts) has a profile with an emoji avatar, bio, beat and stories, and posts opinions (staff heat up to 6); GLSN's crew leads Social's account strip. The preview panel is four GLSN pundits who don't write stories (writers and panel are kept separate, plus the rotating guest stars): Brannew (insider), Crocus (columnist), Shakky (gambler), Professor Clover (analytics); they took over Dana Whitfield's, Marcus Pell's, Sal Benedetto's and Dr. Nina Kessler's seats in 2026 week 12 (`SEAT`), inheriting each seat's record and berries; old previews keep the old names. Anyone can lock: Shakky the most but not every game, the others now and then for their own reasons (a hunch, a revenge game, something Brannew heard, an upset), not only on the numbers. Guest stars (`GUESTS`, `guestFor` in `news.js`; `GUEST_BIO`, `LEGENDS`, `guestRecord` in `site/index.html`): parody One Piece characters from across the series, never quoting it. Crew guests (Straw Hats plus Bartolomeo, Koby, Smoker, Perona, Law, Hancock, Buggy) take every third regular-season week (2, 5, 8, 11, 14); legends (Shanks, Whitebeard, Garp, Mihawk, Kizaru, Big Mom) come only in playoff weeks as a gold Special Guest. Both lists are shuffled per season (seeded by the year), so nobody repeats within a season; Sonny wants them to feel special, so keep them occasional. They pick every game in character, may post that week, and have their own record on their profiles, kept out of Panel Standings and the berry bets. Social posts have kinds (fan, pundit, reporter, team, league), a heat score that drives likes and virality (how inflammatory or funny, not how important: official accounts stay 1 to 3 and never go viral, trolls and hot takes from fans run up to 10; `buzz` in `site/index.html` weights heat far above account size), and a time; a post appears only once its time has passed. Tapping a name, @ or avatar opens a profile (`#u-<handle>`, `userView`): bio, followers (a seeded base by kind that grows with the square root of likes earned, with the week's gain), following, every post with the most liked pinned; team accounts link their manager with the season record and bounty, panelists show their picks and berry bankroll, fans show "Fan of" when their posts clearly back one team. Social opens with one swipeable account strip (`accountStrip`: GLSN's crew, then every team's official account) and keeps Who to Follow (top official accounts and fans) in a sticky right column on desktop, inside the feed after the fifth post on phones. Team accounts use the team's logo as their avatar (`teamAva`, falling back to the initials square if it's ESPN's default or doesn't load); `fetch.js` saves each team's logo to `archive/logos/<year>_<id>.<ext>` and the site serves it from `logos/`, since ESPN's custom-upload links (mystique-api) don't load on other sites. Each team has one account: posts under a second handle merge into the first (`ACCT_ALIAS`). Followers: a base by kind (league 48k, reporter 26k, team 16k, pundit 2.5k, fan 120) plus a kind weight times the square root of likes, so one viral post doesn't outgrow a team. Fans and reporters keep letter avatars.

## Models

The decisions and backtests are logged in the "BFL Model Log" Google Doc in Sonny's Drive; add an entry whenever a model changes.

- Next week's win chance: ESPN's projection for the lineup as set (holes count as zero). Backtest on 2023 to 2025 (127 games): 68% of winners picked, Brier 0.206, against 55% and 0.254 for the original model.
- Season odds (`odds.js`): 5,000 simulated seasons; later weeks use a blend of lineup projection and points per game (recent weeks weighted 0.8 per week), minus NFL bye losses, with a wider spread; ties go to head-to-head then points for (the league's ESPN setting is `H2H_RECORD`).
- The model is nicknamed Luffy (after One Piece) in the stories.
- Player simulation (`players.js`): ESPN projection times a ratio drawn from each position's real distribution, player-specific spread, and same-game links (receivers with their QB, defenses against the offense they face). Produces this week's win chances, live win chances and Ones to Watch. A player ruled out (OUT or IR) during his game counts as having nothing left to play; a game is exactly 100% or 0% only once every starter on both sides is done.
- Laboon, the manager ratings (Elo, `site/index.html`, Managers > Laboon; named after the One Piece whale): all finished games since 2023, wins only, K 24 (1.5x in the winners' bracket), halfway back to 1500 each season. Backtest on 2023 to 2025 (136 games): 58% of winners, Brier 0.246; a résumé, not a forecast.
- Awards (own top tab, Players and Managers sub-tabs with a season picker; `seasonAwards` in `site/index.html`): One Piece-named player awards for every season (Pirate King MVP, The One Piece Bowl MVP, Nami QB, Sanji RB, Zoro WR, Franky TE, Usopp K, Jinbe D/ST, Robin pickup, Thousand Sunny trade, Chopper comeback, Brook bench, Buggy bust, Ace donut, Foxy worst drop, Karoo player of the week); Foxy is the most a dropped player scored as a starter for other teams, charged to the team that dropped him (shown as "Dropped by", pays no bounty); Karoo goes to the most weeks as Karoo's Player of the Week (each NFL week's top-scoring starter, `karooWeeks`, also the recap's weekly award and a count on player pages); the current season shows "so far". A player's points count on every team he started for; the award is credited to the team that has him now (or had him last that season), in every season. A ring goes to a player with 3+ starts for the champion that season or a start in one of its playoff games; kickers and defenses for starting the Bowl or at least half the regular season (no streamer rings; player pages only, not lists).
- Sonny wants every article page to look as close to a Wikipedia article as possible (infobox, lead, sections, tables, see also).
- Manager pages (`mgrWiki`) and player pages (`wikiHtml`, `seasonStory`) read like wiki articles: an infobox, a lead written from the numbers, then the story (the Opus career profile for managers, a templated paragraph per season for players: how he arrived, points and rank, his best game with any record it set (`gameRecords`: the most by his position all-time or that season), Karoo weeks, the close win he carried, his playoff games, career milestones every 500 points as a starter (`careerMilestones`), donuts, moves (right after his totals; a trade links GLSN's story and tells his starts before and after it and what the other side got from its return; vetoed trades he was in, linked to the veto story), awards, Bounty Board and the finish of the team he ended the season with). ## Conventions

- Plain JavaScript (Node 20 in Actions), no framework; the site is one file, `site/index.html`.
- Match the surrounding code: short comments that say why, early returns, compact helpers.
- Commit messages: a short summary line, then a few lines on what and why.
- The site defaults to dark mode with a light toggle; check both when changing colors. Gold marks the Buckaroo Bowl; winners in brackets are outlined in white.
